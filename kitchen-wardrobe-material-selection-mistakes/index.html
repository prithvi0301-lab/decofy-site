Mistakes to avoid when choosing kitchen and wardrobe materials

Most material-selection regrets come from comparing the wrong things — mismatched quote scopes, unasked questions about water or termite risk, or a decision made on finish alone. Here are the common mistakes to check for before you commit.

Discuss your project

8 mistakes homeowners make

1. Choosing a material without checking water exposure

Water exposure varies by location — a sink zone, utility area or humid room needs different questions asked than a dry bedroom wall. Confirm what's suitable for where the piece will actually sit.

2. Not asking about termite risk for enclosed spaces

Termite risk is worth checking for any enclosed or long-term storage piece, not just areas with obvious water exposure. Ask about termite resistance alongside water resistance.

3. Comparing quotes with different scopes

Ask exactly what's included in each quote — hardware, edge finishing, services and accessories can vary between suppliers. Compare the same scope before comparing the price.

4. Choosing based on finish alone

A finish can be replicated across several base materials. Confirm what's underneath the finish before deciding.

5. Ignoring hardware and accessories

Hinges, channels and drawer mechanisms are worth comparing alongside the panel material — ask what's included and what it's rated for.

6. Not checking exactly what the warranty covers

Confirm the scope, duration and exclusions of any warranty before approving a project, rather than assuming full coverage.

7. Not asking what could affect the installation schedule

Ask your supplier what site factors — such as access, timing or pending works — could affect the schedule before you approve a start date.

8. Not confirming what "waterproof" or "termite-proof" actually means

Confirm what the material actually is and what the warranty covers, rather than relying on the label alone.

Action checklist

  • List where the piece will sit and what it needs to resist (water, termites).
  • Ask for the material specification, not just the material name.
  • Request quotes with an identical scope — hardware, accessories, edges and services.
  • Confirm warranty coverage and duration in writing.
  • Ask what site factors could affect the installation schedule.

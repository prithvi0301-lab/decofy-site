uPVC vs plywood: which is better for your kitchen or wardrobe?

Short answer: it depends on the room. In a kitchen, water usually decides. In a wardrobe, termites and damp walls usually matter more than spills. Decofy's uPVC panels are 100% waterproof. Kitchen cabinets or wardrobe: what to compare

Kitchen cabinets: start with water

Cabinets around the sink face spills, steam and daily wiping, and base units sit close to a wet floor, so water is the first thing to compare. Decofy builds its uPVC modular kitchens in Bengaluru from uPVC panels that are 100% waterproof.

If you are weighing plywood, ask which grade is offered, how cut edges and joints around the sink are sealed, and what the warranty covers.

For a closer look, read our guide to waterproof kitchen cabinet materials.

Wardrobes: start with termites and damp

Wardrobes are enclosed storage, so spills matter less. Termites, and damp from outer walls or a neighbouring bathroom, matter more. Decofy's uPVC wardrobes in Bengaluru use uPVC panels that are termite-proof.

If you are weighing plywood, ask whether it is treated against termites, what treatment is used, and what the supplier's warranty covers.

For a closer look, read our guide to termite-proof wardrobe materials.

Planning both? A kitchen and wardrobes can be planned together as one uPVC interiors project, or separately.

What to check, whichever material you choose

  • Which parts of the cabinet use the stated material: carcass, shutters and shelves?
  • How are cut edges and joints sealed, especially near the sink?
  • What hardware and internal accessories are included?
  • What does the warranty cover and exclude?
  • How will site measurements and service points be verified?
  • What work must be complete before installation begins?
  • What day-to-day cleaning and care does the finish need?
  • Can you see the exact finish in person before you decide?

Material alone does not decide quality. Layout, hardware, build accuracy, edge details, installation and after-sales terms still matter. See common material-selection mistakes to avoid.
